Distill

(brief)

Distill is a Chrome extension that listens to your active tab, translates the audio into 16 languages at once, and streams live ASL visual cards on top of any site. Built in 36 hours at HackAZona, it won the Microsoft Track.

(my role)

I designed the whole interface in the 36-hour window: the extension panel, the language tokens, the toggles, the floating captions, and the ASL card system, then tested it live on real lectures while my three teammates built the Azure pipeline.

(the problem)

Live audio locks people out.

Lectures, meetings, and videos happen in real time, and if you are deaf, hard of hearing, or learning the language, you are behind from the first sentence. Captions, when they exist, come in one language and never in ASL.

Behind the scenes...

Chrome extension

Translate any tab in real time

An extension has almost no room, so every component earns its place. Input language is one clear button group, outputs are multi-select tokens color-coded per language, speaker diarization and floating captions are plain toggles, and a single blue CTA starts everything.

The hard constraint was speed of reading: this UI is used while someone is talking. I sized the type, contrast, and spacing for high-speed readability, then floated the panel over YouTube, Zoom, Meet, and Teams without masking a single video control.

The pipeline

From tab audio to ASL cards

Live tab audio streams to Azure Speech Translation, comes back as text and synthesized speech in the target language, and the panel updates in real time while ASL cards populate alongside, so a signing user and a translating user watch the same lecture together.

We tested it live on a Chinese lecture during the hackathon to prove the multilingual output holds up in context, not just in a demo script.
