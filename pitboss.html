Pit Boss

An in-store display for Pit Boss grills, designed for Dansons. It turns a grill sitting on a shop floor into a place that explains the grill.

A sponsored studio project at ASU. It received the Design Excellence Award for Fall 2024.

Company
Dansons × ASU
Tools
Sketching, cardboard models, 3D rendering
Role
Industrial Design student
Year
2024

What I saw in the store

Dansons, the company behind Pit Boss, asked our studio to look at how their grills are sold. So I started where the customer starts, on the shop floor.

At Lowe's, the Pit Boss grill sat on the floor and taught customers nothing. There was a help button, but the worker it called took a while to arrive.

The Pit Boss section itself was pushed to the back of the store, by the bathroom, under dark lighting. A person could walk past a very good grill and never learn what it does.

Four ideas

Before settling on one direction I sketched four, each solving a different moment between the shelf and the first cook.

Boss Ball is an expandable box. Grills on the Wheels gives the packaging pull-down wheels, so one person can move it. Mini Boss turns the packaging into a cardboard grill for kids. Boss Grip is a removable grip that makes the box easier to carry.

Sketching all four showed me where the biggest gap was. It was not the box. It was the moment in the store when nobody explains the grill.

Planning the display

So the direction became a display that stands around the grill and does the explaining a salesperson isn't there to do.

I drew the layout first, then built it small in cardboard. Cardboard is quick, and it let me check heights, sight lines and where a person would actually stand.

The graphics

The panels say one thing clearly: five ways to cook in one grill. I wanted a shopper to get that in the two seconds they spend walking past.

A blue pattern ties the pieces together, so the section reads as one place and can be spotted from across the store.

The scale model

I built a scale model with the graphics applied, to see how the panels, shelves and grill sit together from each side.

Walking around a model shows things a drawing hides, like which panel blocks which, and what you see first from the aisle.

The final display

The grill stands in the middle, with the story of what it does around it.

Accessories and pellets sit within reach, so the questions a shopper has, what it cooks, what it needs, what it costs to run, are answered in one spot.

Presenting it

We presented the work at the end of the semester.

The project received the Design Excellence Award for Fall 2024 from the industrial design program at ASU.

What I learned

  • Going to the store shows things a brief never mentions.
  • A product is first experienced on the shop floor, before anyone uses it.
  • A cardboard model answers questions a render can't.

How I'll use it next

  • Start projects by watching the real setting first.
  • Treat the first screen of an app the way I treated this display.
