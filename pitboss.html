Pit Boss

An in-store display for Pit Boss grills, for Dansons. It turns a grill sitting on a shop floor into a place that explains what the grill does.It’s not just a grill. It’s Pit Boss.

Year
Fall 2024
Client
Dansons × ASU
Role
Industrial Design student
Process
Field research, sketching, cardboard, rendering

01

What I saw in the store

At Lowe’s, the Pit Boss grill sat on the floor and taught customers nothing. The help button called a worker who took a while to arrive.

The section was pushed to the back of the store by the bathroom, under dark lighting.

02

Four ideas

Boss Ball, an expandable box. Grills on the Wheels, packaging with pull-down wheels. Mini Boss, a cardboard grill for kids made from the packaging. Boss Grip, a removable carry grip.

The biggest gap wasn’t the box. It was the moment in the store when nobody explains the grill.

03

Planning the display

I drew the layout, then built it small in cardboard to check heights, sight lines and where a shopper would stand.

The graphics say one thing clearly: five ways to cook in one grill.

04

The scale model

Walking around a model showed what a drawing hides: which panel blocks which, and what you see first from the aisle.

05

The final display

The grill stands in the middle with the story of what it does around it, and the accessories within reach.

Recognition

What I learned

  • Going to the store shows things a brief never mentions.
  • A cardboard model answers questions a render can’t.

How I'll use it next

  • Start by watching the real setting first.
  • Treat the first screen of an app the way I treated this display.
