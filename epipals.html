Epi Pals

Confidence to keep playing

A watch for children with epilepsy that helps them feel confident and independent, and gives their parents quiet reassurance.

01Millions of children live with epilepsy

In 2021, an estimated 18.15 million children and adolescents worldwide were living with epilepsy. The same three problems came up again and again.

18.15millionchildren and adolescents living with epilepsy worldwide in 2021
3recurring problems: children feel restricted, parents worry, no support after a seizure

02What I heard

I interviewed Cherly, a student whose seizures began at age 10. She can't drive or be alone, and takes medication every day.

Medical devices felt annoying and embarrassing to her. She wanted epilepsy not to define her: to feel normal, blend in, and still be safe.

“I stopped wearing my medical alert bracelet because it got annoying.”

Cherly, young adult and student, York, PA
Goals

Safety without fuss

Automatic seizure logging, location sharing with family during an episode, and alerts that vibrate rather than beep.

Frustrations

Help comes too late

She can't get help fast enough during an aura, and medical devices feel like a label.

Motivations

To blend in

Independence, and a life that epilepsy doesn't define.

03Beyond detection

Existing products detect seizures, monitor body signals and show data to caregivers. None of them help the child feel good wearing one.

So I asked: how might we help kids with epilepsy feel confident and independent, while reassuring their parents?

04Friendly, not medical

The direction was soft silicone and a playful character: fun, cool and friendly, never medical looking.

Small ears and a face make the watch a companion, and a family of colours lets each child pick the one that feels like theirs.

05Support that stays quiet

Five layers sit inside: interface, processing, connectivity and location, haptic feedback and sensing.

A gentle one, two, three vibration guides breathing when a child feels confused or overwhelmed. It needs no conscious interaction and stays private.

Haptics

Grounding, not alarms

A 1, 2, 3 pattern the child feels rather than hears. Calm, private and not disruptive.

Location

Trust without hovering

Live location and updates for parents, so there are fewer check-in texts.

After

Something to hold onto

Nothing to tap. Just something familiar after a seizure.

06Colour, material and finish

A translucent ribbed strap and a soft-edged body, in sage, lilac and sky. Bright enough to be chosen, calm enough to wear every day.

07In a child's day

It alerts before a seizure, the child finds a safe spot, and haptics help afterwards. There is nothing to tap, just something familiar to hold onto.

Epi Pals

Talking to one real person changed the brief more than any market data.

What I learned

  • For children, how a device feels to wear matters as much as what it does.
  • Haptics can comfort without words or screens.

Next time

  • Interview the people who will live with a product before sketching.
  • Design assistive products to be wanted, not just tolerated.
