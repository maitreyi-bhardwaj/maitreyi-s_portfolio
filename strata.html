Strata

The thinking mat

A floor mat that reads your body and adjusts under your head, neck, upper back and lower back, so you can actually rest.

Project
Solo concept
Year
2026
Role
Industrial designer
Tools
Sketching, clay, 3D modelling, rendering

01People don't get time to rest

People who work at screens all day rarely get real rest, and the body carries it. I could not find a product designed to give the body that recovery time back.

73%of workers say they don't get enough time to rest each day (WHO 2023)
11hrsof screen and work time a day projected for knowledge workers by 2040
0products designed to give the body back the recovery time it no longer gets

02Designing for Priya, 31

A remote worker who spends ten hours a day working from her apartment floor, with lower back and neck pain and no ergonomic setup.

She already works from the floor, so that is where I put the answer.

03From sticky notes to clay

I started wide, with quick sketches of furniture and spaces, then narrowed to a surface you lie on.

The form began as ridges and valleys shaped by hand in clay. I drew small figures on the photos to see how a body would rest on each one.

04Finding the surface

Early versions were islands of shapes. The later ones follow the outline of a body, so you know where to lie without being told.

05Four zones, four shapes

Each part of the body needs something different, so each zone has its own form.

Head

Round dome

Takes the load off the muscles at the base of the skull, which carry three to five times their normal load from screen posture.

Neck

Saddle channel

Keeps the natural curve of the neck, so it is not compressed while you rest.

Upper back

Two ridges

Support the muscles on either side of the spine. The spine itself never touches the surface.

Lower back

Raised dome

Fills the 20 to 40 mm gap under the lower back, so those muscles can stop working.

06Lie down. It reads. You recover.

Twelve pressure zones read how you are lying. A small controller sets how firm each zone should be, and a dial lets you override it. It can also take heart-rate data from a smartwatch.

Car seats already adjust their lumbar support this way. I moved the idea from the car to the floor.

Sense

12 pressure zones

Read every 200 milliseconds, with heart-rate variability from a smartwatch.

Decide

An ESP32 controller

Combines pressure and biometrics into a setting for each zone: idle, passive, adaptive or rigid.

Override

A dial

Head, neck, upper back or lower back. You stay in control.

07Six layers, every one already exists

I only used materials and processes that are made today, because a concept is more useful when someone could build it. I estimated it could sell for $299 to $349.

01

Knit skin

Breathable polyester-spandex that stretches over the terrain.

02

TPU lattice

3D printed: dense under the ridges, open under the valleys.

03

EVA foam inserts

Soft at the head, firm at the lower back.

04

Sensor grid

A thin pressure film, printed like a keyboard membrane.

05

ESP32 and Bluetooth

The brain, in a housing at the edge.

06

Silicone base

Grips the floor and keeps the cold out.

08A surface that works while you rest

It lies on the floor like a rug, in three colours, and asks nothing of you but to lie down.

Strata

A concept, not yet built or tested. The next step is to make one zone and put a body on it.

What I learned

  • A form has to be justified zone by zone, not as one shape.
  • Using parts that already exist keeps a concept believable.
