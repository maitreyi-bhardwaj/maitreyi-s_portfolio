Vibin

Live social discovery

People at a venue post the vibe in real time, GenAI turns it into data, and the map shows what is actually happening right now. Co-founded, designed and shipped to the App Store.

ProblemFive apps to make one plan

To decide where to go tonight, people check the map, Instagram, stories, reviews and group chats, and still don't know what a place feels like right now. Photos are old, reviews are older. The live vibe is nowhere.

Our research showed the decision runs on FOMO and spontaneity, not on star ratings.

The work ran in four stages: grounding and alignment, discover, design, launch.

PivotFrom a calendar to a vibe

An early direction looked like an event app: search, featured events, a calendar, a price list. It answered when and how much, not what a place feels like.

So the first question on screen became "What is your vibe?". The product moved from utility to emotion, and category filters gave way to moods.

Information architectureTwo structures, and users picked one

There were two honest ways to build this, so I built both. One organised everything around places: open a map, browse venues. The other around people: a feed of who is out tonight.

Places won, clearly. People opened the app asking "where is good right now", not "who is out". So the map became home, filtered by vibe instead of category, because nobody chooses a night out by the word "bar".

The maps also break a vibe into fields, so it can be scored and searched: type of place, date and time, song, VCS, energy, crowd and aesthetic, down to lighting, dress code and colour palette.

First directionDark, and built around the map

The first high-fidelity version was called Vibe Check. It was almost monochrome, opened on a dark map, and pinned each venue to it with a live count in VCS.

The number was the biggest thing on the venue screen, larger than the name. That idea survived every later version.

Second directionLighter, and led by photos

The next version moved to white and electric blue, and the map stopped being the first thing you see. Home became a scroll of real photos of places, each with its mood and VCS on the image.

The Vibe Report became its own deep navy screen with circular stats, an energy slider from Dull to Electric, a colour palette and the song playing.

Name and logoThree names, one V

The product changed name twice, and the identity changed with it.

Vibe Check began as a plain letter V, then the V became a tick cut out of a gradient circle: a check mark and a V at once.

Pulse dropped the mark for a tall condensed wordmark in deep blue, with a voice of its own: "Some places breathe. Others buzz. Pulse helps you hear them."

Vibin kept the best of both: the V tick in its circle, set beside a tall condensed wordmark.

ColourThree dark themes, then light

I tested the home screen on navy, indigo and near black, alongside white.

Vibin shipped light, with one blue for action (#1877F2) and one pink for feeling (#FF6B9D), so the photos of places carry the rest of the colour.

TypographyOne headline, tested until it spoke

The headline is the only sentence on the home screen, so I tried it in different typefaces and line breaks.

The shipped version is light on the first line and semibold on the second, with the word Vibes in pink.

Venue cardTrading the star rating for a live score

The first card was a white panel with a 4.6 star rating, which says what a place was like over years. The final card puts the vibe score on the photo and a blue panel below it with the name, the distance and who checked in recently.

Final designOnboarding

Five short screens explain the whole idea: explore, see where the motion is, post, find your crew, get deals. The last one is the reason to come back: time-bound deals from clubs, cafes and restaurants, redeemed with a QR code.

  1. Explore places and events
  2. See where the motion is
  3. Post and share vibes
  4. Connect with your crew
  5. Get exclusive deals

Vibe ReportA live report for every venue

Posting a vibe takes seconds: one photo, one line. GenAI classifies the atmosphere into structured data, so "packed rooftop, live music" becomes something you can search and filter.

Every venue gets a Vibe Report: a scorecard built from what people posted in the last hour, not last year. Crowd level, music, how photo-worthy it is, and an AI read on the atmosphere.

Profile and rewardsPosting earns status

VCS is also the reward. Every profile shows saved places, check-ins and VCS, and a level bar: Level 7 Viber, 150 VCS to Level 8. Venues, Activity and Rewards sit in three tabs below.

For venuesThe other side of the marketplace

Venues get their own dashboard. Total views, saves and check-ins sit at the top, each with its weekly change. They choose whether they appear as a venue or a time-based event, manage events, and can send a push notification to Vibin users.

Brand in the worldA brand for nightlife, not software

Vibin had to feel like the streets, not like an app. I designed every asset in-house: event posters, vendor flyers, merch and social content, all from the same gradient-and-glow system as the app.

Business modelA marketplace that charges no one

Vendors get visibility and foot traffic. Users get live vibes and deals at the door. Eventbrite charges 3.5% plus $1.59 a ticket and Posh takes 15%. Vibin charges neither side.

Shipped applicationShipped, then learned from real use

Vibin is live on the App Store as "Find vibes, unlock local deals". Post-launch behaviour invalidated three assumptions from our interviews, so I decided each next change from what people did, not what they said.
